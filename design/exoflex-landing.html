Investigational device · Upper-limb rehabilitation · Sherbrooke, Québec

A portable upper-limb device that reads the movement a patient is attempting, and adds only the assistance that movement is missing.

ExoFlex fuses surface EMG, inertial measurement and force sensing under an Assist-As-Needed control strategy, so support scales to effort in real time — on a clinic table, or in a living room after discharge.

01 — Context

Upper-limb recovery after a stroke is measured in months. Supervised therapy is usually measured in weeks, and the equipment that makes high-repetition practice possible stays behind in the clinic.

Portability moves the constraint. When the device leaves with the patient, the amount of practice available is governed by the person's own capacity and their clinician's plan — not by the room the equipment happens to live in.

03 — Mechanism

Four parts of one system.

01 / Intent detection

The device waits to be asked.

Surface EMG electrodes on the forearm register the muscle activation that precedes visible movement. Inertial measurement units track segment orientation, and force sensors at the cuff measure what the limb is actually delivering. Fusing the three streams lets the controller separate an attempted movement from tremor, noise, or the passive weight of the arm.

02 / Assist-As-Needed

Support is the deficit, not the motion.

The controller compares the movement being attempted against the trajectory that movement requires, and closes only the difference. As the user's own contribution rises, assistance falls, which keeps the person doing the work the device exists to make possible. Torque and range are bounded at every step of the loop.

03 / Portability

Therapy follows the patient.

The system is worn rather than mounted, runs from an onboard battery, and is fitted without a technician present. Practice is no longer tied to a fixed frame in a treatment room, which puts the schedule back in the hands of the clinician and the household rather than the equipment.

04 / Session data

Every session leaves a record.

Repetitions, active range, effort distribution and the quantity of assistance supplied are captured per session. The clinician reads a measurable trace between appointments instead of a recollection at the next one, and can adjust the plan against what actually happened.

04 — Control strategy

Assistance is the difference, not the movement.

07 — Origin

Nine students, one capstone, 2023.

ExoFlex began as a capstone engineering project at Université de Sherbrooke: nine students, one prototype, and an academic deadline. It became a company because the clinicians who handled that prototype asked when they could use it on a caseload. The founding team still does the engineering.

The discipline matters as much as the mechanism. Risk management follows ISO 14971, and a quality management system is maintained alongside development rather than assembled after the fact — because a device that goes home with a patient has to be defensible before it is impressive.

ExoFlex is an investigational device. It is not cleared, licensed or approved for sale by Health Canada, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration, or any other regulatory authority, and it is not available for commercial distribution. Descriptions on this page refer to device function and to validation work in progress. Nothing here is a claim of clinical benefit, treatment outcome, cure, or regulatory clearance. Survey figures reflect clinician-reported perceptions, not measured clinical effectiveness. Any use of the device occurs under the supervision of a qualified clinician within an approved study protocol.
